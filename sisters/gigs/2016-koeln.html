2016: Cologne

8 March 2016
Palladium · Cologne, Germany
Support: LSDonCIA

It took three attempts. After the Cologne show had been cancelled in October and again in December 2015 because Andrew Eldritch was suffering from acute laryngitis, it finally went ahead. I had already written a small declaration of love to The Sisters Of Mercy in 2014. But how would the audience react after two cancellations?

For some, the decision was made for them, as the Sisters’ visit coincided with a wave of flu in North Rhine-Westphalia. Those who made it to the Palladium were treated to even more fog than usual. And would Andrew’s voice hold up? It became clear that he was particularly cautious with the higher notes. The set itself opened with fast, powerful numbers. “Body Electric” in particular is a great song for getting the crowd going. Other highlights for me were “Lucretia”, “Summer” and “Flood II”.

Some members of the audience who had only followed the Sisters more casually had quite a bit of trouble recognising many of the newly reworked classics straight away …

“Lucretia”, “Temple Of Love” and “First And Last And Always” in particular had been extensively reworked to bring them closer to the Sisters’ sound of recent years. It was a shame, though, that so little of the wealth of “new” material was played. “Summer”, “Arms” and “Crash And Burn” made it into the setlist — but “Far Parade”, written in 2009 and with its lyrics available on the band’s website, is sadly still waiting for its live debut.

Opinions about the gig were divided on the forums, including poison-door.net. Leaving aside the rather thin sound in the Palladium at times, there was a band on stage in good spirits and clearly enjoying playing. Chris Catalyst and Ben Christo really put their foot down during the encores. Towards the end of the gig, Andrew Eldritch rested his head on Christo’s shoulder — was he relieved that his slightly battered voice had made it through the 90 minutes in one piece? Like a footballer returning to the starting eleven after an injury lay-off?

Who knows. I almost didn’t care how the gig would turn out — my main concern was that, after the cancellations, the band should show up for the fans and make it clear that they were still a force to be reckoned with. For me, the Sisters delivered a solid gig with no surprises and a nicely balanced set. It is also a lovely opportunity to catch up with people from the forums and mailing lists, to talk about the band, old material and the effect of the new guitars on the live sound. I, for one, would like to see the Sisters again soon — and who knows, perhaps next time the Tilburg show from 2011 will finally be knocked off the top of my all-time gig list…
