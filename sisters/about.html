A personal retrospective

More than thirty years have passed since the Sisters first came together in Leeds. Independent, almost started as a joke – Andrew Eldritch and Gary Marx wanted to hear themselves on the radio, and John Peel duly obliged – the band went through a number of different incarnations. And with them came different styles. There was the layered sound of Wayne Hussey’s twelve-string guitar, which took the Sisters’ music to another level. Then came the pop-duo era, which firmly established Eldritch as a singer-songwriter. After that, the swaggering rock ego trip with Andreas Bruhn, Tony James and Tim Bricheno. The albumless but constantly songwriting line-up built around Adam Pearson, later reinforced by Chris Starling. And now there is the mixture of metal, alternative and pop that Chris Catalyst and Ben Christo bring to the stage.

For a long time now, the Sisters have no longer really been about a new album. By the early 2000s, as their live activity increased again, people were regularly asking about a new record. The deal with EastWest had ended in 1997, and since then countless reasons had been given for why there were no new studio-produced songs: they had waited too long with the independent single ‘Summer’; studio time and marketing cost a great deal of money; and, in any case, neither the music piracy that flourished around Napster in the 2000s nor the crisis it helped trigger in the music industry made the prospect of risking – and potentially losing – money on a new record particularly attractive.

Another factor was that, from 1996 onwards, the Sisters reinvented themselves as a live band. Without a label, but with new songs. The 30th anniversary tour in 2011 took the Sisters across several continents, including South Africa and Japan for the first time. New songs were cheerfully slipped into the set, while older material was reinterpreted. Seeing the Sisters live is not watching a nostalgia act, nor is it simply old soup poured into new tins.

I have been seeing the Sisters in their various incarnations, in different countries, since 2000. And there is more to it than that. As early as 1998, the ‘hunt’ for unreleased material had begun. Someone called ‘Matti’ had uploaded MP3s from the bootleg *Visions at the Forum* to his T-Online homepage. Suddenly, new songs such as ‘Summer’, ‘(We Are The Same) Suzanne’ and ‘Romeo Down’ were available.

The Dominion mailing list – and later MyHeartland as a forum – brought fans together and encouraged the trading of bootlegs, first on CD-ROMs and later digitally. That meant I fairly quickly got hold of recordings from 1999, when Eldritch, Pearson and Mike Varjak moved on from a festival in Portugal to ‘To The Planet Edge’ and toured North America.

It was an exciting time, culminating – at least for me – at the M’era Luna Festival in 2000. I met so many people from ‘Dominion’ there, as well as from ‘Poison Door’, the German-language forum. It was simply mad. Borders no longer seemed to matter, whether you were trading recordings with fans in England or finally putting faces to the various forum nicknames you had come across at concerts.

Since then, plenty of concerts and experiences have been added to the story – sometimes more of them, as between 2001 and 2003, sometimes fewer. The fact that the Sisters will probably never record and release new songs in a studio again? Fair enough.

The excitement when a tour is announced, or when there is a concert somewhere nearby, is just as strong in 2014 as it was in 2000. The pleasure of seeing familiar faces in the crowd; the stories people tell; hearing what has happened in their lives; remembering the things you experienced together – all of that turns a journey to a gig into something special. I would not want to miss it.

I remember M’era Luna in 2000, and ‘raise your arms’ during ‘Flood II’. I remember Hamburg in 2001, when I met Vicus on the train near Osnabrück, when we had a get-together with other fans in the Irish pub opposite the Große Freiheit, and when I later ended up somewhere in Altona, once again with Vicus, carrying on the night.

Or 2003, when I visited Vanessa in England and saw the Sisters on two nights in their home city of Leeds – and did *not* meet Eldritch in the toilets, despite what Paul later liked to claim. 😉

Or RockTernat in 2001, when I travelled right across Brussels to meet Inga from Poland and other fans at the gig near a Brussels garden centre. Or the trips around the Netherlands with Chris in 2011 and 2014.

It no longer matters whether the Sisters will ever release another record.

I am looking forward to the next gig, somewhere, sometime – to the full-on sound, the fog, the lights, the people, and the new stories.

And, as always, I will be grinning.

Written: 28 November 2014.
